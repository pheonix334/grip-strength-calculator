How to test each way

1Dynamometer: sit upright, elbow bent at 90°, forearm neutral, and squeeze as hard as you comfortably can for 2–3 seconds. Repeat 2–3 times per hand and use your best reading.
2Dead hang: grab a pull-up bar with an overhand grip, hang with straight arms and feet off the ground, and time until you have to let go.
3Hand gripper: using a spring-loaded gripper, find the heaviest resistance you can fully close (handles touching) with good form, one hand at a time.
4Whichever method you use, stay consistent between tests so your saved history reflects real progress, not measurement differences.

Why grip strength matters

Grip strength is more than just how hard you can squeeze something. Research in aging and sports science has repeatedly found it correlates with overall muscular strength, functional independence in older adults, and general physical resilience — which is why it's used as a simple, low-cost screening measure in frailty and longevity research.

For athletes and lifters, grip strength is also a practical limiting factor in exercises like deadlifts, pull-ups, and farmer's carries, so tracking it over time can be a useful training metric on its own.

One reading is just a snapshot — it doesn't diagnose any condition. If your strength has dropped noticeably and unexpectedly, that's worth mentioning to a doctor.

Three ways to test your grip

Dynamometer: the standard clinical/research tool. Most precise, but not something most people own.

Dead hang: hang from a pull-up bar with straight arms and time how long you last. A practical proxy for grip endurance if you have access to a bar.

Hand gripper: spring-loaded grip tools rated by resistance (commonly in lb) — use the heaviest one you can fully close. Widely available and cheap, popular in strength-training communities.

These three methods measure related but not identical things (peak force vs. endurance vs. closing strength), so benchmarks are method-specific — that's why this calculator scores each one separately rather than converting between them.
